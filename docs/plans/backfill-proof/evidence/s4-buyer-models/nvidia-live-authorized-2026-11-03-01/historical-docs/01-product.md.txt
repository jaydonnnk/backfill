# Product: Show what Backfill adds to Muse

## Problem

Muse already helps people find dining deals. Backfill must show why separate
customers benefit from joining a café's conditional offer, and what the AI
actually contributes. A replay of increasing booking counts does not answer
either question on its own.

Our target is independent parties choosing the same café and off-peak slot.
They approve their own terms and need not organize together or share a table.
The café controls the minimum group, capacity, deadline and acceptable price.

## Success metric

Primary product metric: incremental confirmed covers under the same
merchant-approved conditional offer, pooled versus separate bookings.
Report the difference only after the deadline and capacity checks, with the
same buyer requests in both cases. Planned acceptance example: four eligible
two-person parties, an eight-person minimum and eight seats produce eight
confirmed covers together and zero separately. This is an illustrative test,
not a forecast of incremental real-world demand or revenue.

An insufficient group must produce zero confirmed covers. Report simulated
savings separately from money collected. Real-world commercial benefit remains
unverified without actual merchant and diner participation.

## Announcement draft

Backfill lets Muse users qualify together for a café's off-peak offer.
Each party approves its own booking, while the café sets the conditions.
The demo shows the buyer's request, the agent's proposal, the price checks and
the resulting group outcome. It also shows when a group fails and when a price
is refused. After a sellout, Wonder can suggest revised terms for owner review.
Recorded examples and simulated payments are labeled throughout.

## What this build delivers

1. A readable decision trail alongside the existing pool demonstration.
   Show whose action it was, what changed and whether the result is pending,
   confirmed or refused. Distinguish customer instructions from agent decisions.
2. One verified complete journey: menu review, owner terms, customer approval,
   commitment, deadline settlement and savings, then a sellout proposal that
   remains unapplied until owner approval. Missing evidence stays missing.
3. Separate proof of pooling value and AI value. Compare Spark against a fixed
   pricing policy on the same synthetic cases and within identical owner rules.
   Report deals, hypothetical merchant receipts, buyer savings, guard refusals,
   latency and cost. Ties and losses are valid results. An attractive immediate
   discount elsewhere remains a competing choice, not a rigged losing baseline.
4. Final submission documentation that names the solo contributor, identifies
   event-built work and links verified evidence. Do this after the build checks.

## Evidence requirements

- Show only supported links between events. A separate negotiation example
  cannot appear to have caused a booking in a different pool.
- No fabricated model reasoning. Show the actual bounded decision or proposal;
  omit sensitive information and never expose a merchant's private minimum.
- Customer approval is claimed only when its evidence exists. A tool instruction
  asking an agent to seek permission is not proof that a person approved.
- Recorded playback, offline fixtures and fresh activity have distinct labels.
- Show requests separately from confirmed covers and do not oversell capacity.
- A stale or interrupted feed cannot imply a new success.
- When a group expires, clearly state no discounted booking was confirmed.
- An invalid price example is labeled as a test if deliberately injected.
- Evaluation is secondary evidence. It does not clutter the booking journey
  or require rebuilding the removed public comparison dashboard.

## Screens

- mockups/demo.html: proposed addition to the existing demo; switch between
  an eight-cover success, a six-cover expiry and a separate unsafe-price test.
  All amounts and events are illustrative. This is not the production interface.
- Existing merchant review and approval screens are reused. No new merchant
  screen or evaluation dashboard is proposed at this gate.

## Non-goals

No video creation, uploads, actual submission, real payment collection, POS
integration, discount scraping, model providers outside the revised eight-tool
scope or broad redesign.
No claim of inventing group buying, proving Spark superiority, guaranteeing
attendance or making every independent café newly accessible to Muse.

## Basis for the positioning

- [ShopBack's Muse announcement](https://corporate.shopback.com/press/shopback-now-connects-to-muse-from-meta-letting-personal-ai-agents-do-the-saving)
  documents dining discovery and deal claiming.
- [Eatigo](https://help.eatigo.com/hc/en-us/articles/47179220811801-What-is-eatigo)
  documents time-based restaurant discounts.
- [WhyQ](https://www.whyq.sg/groupbuy) documents minimum-group food orders.

The proposed distinction is merchant-controlled dine-in pools across independent
agent users. No exact equivalent was established in the reviewed sources;
that is not proof of universal novelty. Customers may prefer an immediate deal.

## Acceptance before calling the build complete

The complete journey repeats successfully twice in an isolated environment;
insufficient demand, capacity limits, invalid prices and missing approvals are
shown honestly; the comparison publishes outcomes without cherry-picking;
desktop and mobile views remain readable; final submission claims match proof.
Offline proof does not substitute for an unperformed live model or Muse session.
